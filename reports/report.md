## Abstract

Deepfakes manipulate the face, the voice, or both. Most detectors look at only one modality, so
they miss fakes of the other kind and are fragile when a video is re-compressed or degraded. This
project builds an audio-visual deepfake detector with three properties:

- **Multimodal:** a face branch (EfficientNet-B0 on face crops) and a voice branch (ResNet-18 on
  log-mel spectrograms) are combined by late and intermediate fusion.
- **Robust:** it is evaluated, and optionally trained, under JPEG/H.264 compression, blur, noise,
  downscaling, MP3 compression and missing modalities.
- **Explainable:** Grad-CAM shows which face regions and which time–frequency regions drove each
  decision.

The planned training uses FakeAVCeleb, whose labels separate fake video from fake audio, with
ASVspoof 2019 LA pre-training for the voice branch. These real-dataset runs have not yet been
verified. A Gradio demo can analyse uploaded videos after a trained checkpoint is supplied.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Problem

Face-swap, face-reenactment and lip-sync models produce realistic fake faces, and text-to-speech
and voice-conversion models produce convincing cloned voices. Combined, they can impersonate a
person in both channels at once. A practical detector must answer "is this video fake?" even if
only one of the two channels was manipulated. It must also survive the compression applied by
social platforms, and it should give evidence a human can check.

### 1.2 Objectives

1. Detect manipulated faces and manipulated voices, and combine both into one decision.
2. Measure and improve robustness to common real-world degradations.
3. Explain each decision with visual heatmaps.
4. Deliver a reproducible pipeline and an interactive demo.

### 1.3 Contributions of this project

- An end-to-end, tested pipeline: dataset download, identity-wise splits, preprocessing,
  training, evaluation, robustness, explainability, demo and report generation.
- A fair comparison protocol: every model is scored on the same test clips against the same
  overall label ("fake if face OR voice is fake").
- Late fusion with max / noisy-OR rules, motivated by that OR-shaped label, alongside a learned
  intermediate fusion with modality dropout.
- Per-category analysis (RealVideo/FakeVideo × RealAudio/FakeAudio; per ASVspoof attack system).

## 2. Related work

**Visual deepfake datasets.**
- FaceForensics++ (Rössler et al., 2019) established a standard benchmark of four manipulation
  methods at several compression levels. It showed that detectors lose accuracy sharply on
  compressed video.
- Celeb-DF (Li et al., 2020) provides higher-quality face swaps with fewer visible artefacts and is
  widely used for cross-dataset evaluation.
- The DeepFake Detection Challenge dataset (Dolhansky et al., 2020) is large-scale, with diverse
  actors and perturbations.

**Visual detectors.**
- MesoNet (Afchar et al., 2018) and XceptionNet baselines (Chollet, 2017; used in FaceForensics++)
  are classic frame-level CNN detectors.
- EfficientNet (Tan & Le, 2019) backbones were common among strong DFDC entries.
- DeepfakeBench (Yan et al., 2023) unifies many detectors under one protocol. It highlights that
  cross-dataset generalisation remains the main weakness.

**Audio anti-spoofing.**
- ASVspoof 2019 (Wang et al., 2020) provides bona fide and spoofed speech from many TTS and
  voice-conversion systems. Its evaluation set contains attacks unseen in training.
- Spectrogram CNNs and graph-attention models such as AASIST (Jung et al., 2022) are strong
  countermeasures.
- SpecAugment (Park et al., 2019) is a standard augmentation for spectrogram models.

**Audio-visual deepfakes.**
- FakeAVCeleb (Khalid et al., 2021) contains real and fake videos with real and cloned voices.
  Each clip is labelled RealVideo-RealAudio, RealVideo-FakeAudio, FakeVideo-RealAudio or
  FakeVideo-FakeAudio. This makes it possible to measure which modality a detector actually relies on.

**Fusion and missing modalities.**
- Multimodal learning is commonly organised into early, intermediate and late fusion
  (Baltrušaitis et al., 2019).
- ModDrop (Neverova et al., 2016) randomly drops modalities during training so that a model
  degrades gracefully when one is missing.

**Explainability.**
- Grad-CAM (Selvaraju et al., 2017) produces class-discriminative heatmaps from the gradients of
  a convolutional layer.
- Adebayo et al. (2018) proposed sanity checks for such saliency methods.

| Dataset | Role | Labels used |

| FakeAVCeleb v1.2 | main training and test set | video label, audio label, overall label, 4 categories |

- **Splits:** grouped 70/15/15 where no official protocol exists. FakeAVCeleb uses person IDs,
  DFDC uses source-video groups, and ASVspoof keeps its official protocol. Source-video grouping
  alone does not guarantee disjoint person identities. The exact Celeb-DF official test list is
  kept; other videos sharing those test identities are excluded from train/validation.
- **Balancing:** FakeAVCeleb contains 500 real and 19,500 fake videos, so a subset balanced per
  split and category is used (`--per-class`). Training additionally weights the loss by the
  real/fake ratio.

## 4. Method

### 4.1 Preprocessing
- **Frames:** 16 frames sampled uniformly per video.
- **Faces:** detected with YuNet (OpenCV), enlarged by a margin of 1.3 and cropped to 224×224.
  Frames without a detection reuse the previous box.
- **Audio:** extracted with ffmpeg as 16 kHz mono, at most 10 s. It is converted to a 128-bin
  log-mel spectrogram (25 ms window, 10 ms hop) and also stored as a FLAC waveform for the audio
  degradations.

### 4.2 Visual branch
- **Backbone:** EfficientNet-B0, ImageNet-pretrained, applied to each frame.
- **Video score:** frame logits and 1280-d embeddings are averaged over the valid frames.
- **Training:** 8 random frames per video, horizontal flip and brightness/contrast augmentation.
  The backbone is frozen for the first epoch, then trained with AdamW (head learning rate 10×)
  and a cosine schedule.
- **Target:** `video_label`, so a real face with a cloned voice counts as real for this branch.

### 4.3 Audio branch
- **Backbone:** ResNet-18, ImageNet-pretrained, applied to the 4 s log-mel window (repeated to 3
  channels), giving a 512-d embedding.
- **Training:** random 4 s crops and SpecAugment (2 frequency masks of up to 16 bins, 2 time masks
  of up to 40 frames). Pre-trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA, then fine-tuned on FakeAVCeleb.
- **Target:** `audio_label`.

### 4.4 Fusion
- **Overall label:** fake if the face or the voice is fake.
- **Late fusion:** no extra training. Three rules are tried on the validation set: a weighted
  average with the weight searched in steps of 0.05, max, and noisy-OR
  `1 − (1 − p_v)(1 − p_a)`. The rule with the best validation AUC is kept.
- **Intermediate fusion:** the concatenation [visual embedding | audio embedding | two presence
  flags] goes through an MLP (256 hidden units).
  - Both branches start from their best checkpoints and stay frozen for 2 epochs, then everything
    is fine-tuned.
  - **Modality dropout** (p = 0.2) hides one modality of a training sample, so the model also works
    when a clip has no face or no audio track.

### 4.5 Robustness
Seven degradations, each at three severities:

| Modality | Degradation | Severities |
|---|---|---|
| Video | JPEG quality | 90 / 50 / 20 |
| Video | H.264 CRF | 23 / 30 / 40 |
| Video | Gaussian blur σ | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| Video | Gaussian noise σ | 5 / 15 / 30 |
| Video | Downscaling | ×2 / ×3 / ×4 |
| Audio | White noise SNR | 20 / 10 / 5 dB |
| Audio | MP3 bitrate | 64 / 32 / 16 kbit/s |

- Video degradations are applied to the face-crop sequence. This approximates platform
  re-compression of the face region.
- **Missing modalities:** face-only and voice-only conditions.
- **Robust training:** each sample is degraded at random with probability 0.5
  (`--robust-augment`).

### 4.6 Explainability
- Grad-CAM is implemented with forward/backward hooks on the last convolutional block of each
  branch (EfficientNet `bn2`, ResNet `layer4`).
- For the fusion model, the gradient of the fused logit gives heatmaps on all face frames and on
  the spectrogram at once.
- Heatmaps explain the predicted class by default.
- A unit test checks faithfulness on a toy model: the heatmap must cover exactly the input
  region that can change the score.

### 4.7 Implementation
- PyTorch with timm and torchvision, configured for Google Colab T4 training with mixed
  precision on CUDA. Real-data GPU training remains unverified.
- Checkpoints are saved every epoch with resume support, and early stopping uses validation AUC.
- The code has a pytest suite; all numbers are produced by the scripts and notebooks listed in
  the README.

## 5. Experimental setup

- **Metrics:** AUC (primary, threshold-free), EER, accuracy, precision, recall and F1 at a
  threshold of 0.5, and accuracy at the EER threshold.
- **Experiments:**
  1. Visual branch: DFDC and FakeAVCeleb, plus cross-dataset transfer to Celeb-DF.
  2. Audio branch: ASVspoof (per-attack EER) and FakeAVCeleb, with and without ASVspoof pre-training.
  3. Fusion comparison: visual-only, audio-only, late and intermediate fusion on the FakeAVCeleb
     test set, overall label, overall and per category.
  4. Robustness: AUC vs. severity for every degradation, plus missing modality; standard vs.
     robust training.
  5. Explainability: Grad-CAM gallery of confident correct and incorrect predictions per category.

## 7. Discussion and limitations

_[Complete with the observed results.]_ Points to address:

- **Dataset bias:** FakeAVCeleb fakes come from a few generation methods. High in-dataset scores
  do not guarantee detection of new generators. Cross-dataset results quantify this.
- **Compression approximation:** compression is applied to face crops, not to full frames before
  face detection.
- **Short audio window:** the audio model sees a 4 s window. Long clips are judged on their central
  window only.
- **Coarse heatmaps:** Grad-CAM resolution is 7×7 per face and 4×13 per spectrogram. Heatmaps show
  regions, not proof.
- **Compute:** a single Colab T4 restricted backbone size and dataset subsets.
